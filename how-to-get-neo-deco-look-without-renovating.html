How to Get the Neo Deco Look Without Renovating

Neo Deco is modern Art Deco glamour built one piece at a time — brass, marble, velvet and bold geometry in a room that still works on a Tuesday. Nothing here needs a contractor, a permit or a single wall knocked down. Five changes, in this order, will get you most of the way there.

1. Start with one anchor piece

Every Neo Deco room has one piece that does the 1920s work, and everything else plays supporting cast. In a living room that is usually the sofa: a deep velvet seat in emerald or navy with slim gold legs, like the emerald velvet loveseat in our living room roundup. In a dining room it might be a banquette bench; in a bedroom, a velvet headboard. Pick one anchor, in one jewel tone, and stop there — a second anchor in a competing colour is how rooms turn busy.

2. Change the lighting, not the ceiling

Lighting is the fastest Deco signal in a room and it needs no rewiring if you choose plug-in or swap-in pieces. A brass globe pendant over a table, a fluted pendant in a corner, or plug-in sconces flanking a mirror all read instantly as Deco. See the five pieces in our Neo Deco lighting roundup for the shapes that work: globes, sputniks, starbursts and fluted brass.

3. Add brass in small, weekend-sized upgrades

Brass is the metal of Neo Deco — warm, not chrome-cold — and it works best in small doses. Swap cabinet pulls for brushed brass, hang a sunburst or scalloped fan-arch mirror, or add a wireless brass picture light over the art you already own. These are the changes in our brass small upgrades roundup: each one takes under an hour and none of them touches a wall stud.

4. Layer geometry on soft surfaces

Deco is a geometry style: sunbursts, fans, chevrons, trellis and stepped arches. The cheap place to put it is on fabric and walls, not furniture. A navy and gold trellis rug under the sofa, gold-foil geometric pillow covers on plain cushions, and a set of gold starburst wall sculptures above the sofa give you the pattern without a single new piece of furniture. Our living room roundup shows all three working together.

5. Finish with marble and a black-and-gold accent

One stone surface and one black-and-gold moment tie the room to the 1920s. In a bathroom that can be a red marble tray on the vanity with brushed gold hardware, as in our red marble bathroom roundup. In a living room it can be a real marble side table, or a bar cart dressed in black and gold — see our bar cart ideas. One marble piece is enough; two competing stones fight each other.

What not to do

Do not paint everything black and gold, and do not buy a matching five-piece Deco set in one afternoon. Neo Deco works because the glamour sits inside a modern, livable room — plain walls, one jewel tone, warm brass, and geometry in a few deliberate places. If a piece needs explaining, it is probably one piece too many.
